AI as a Thinking Tool

Not just worksheets. Three small tools that make students think like historians.

1. Sliding map 2. Timeline 3. Read, Ask, Decide

AI is often used to make worksheets faster.

This work sample shows another use. A teacher describes a thinking tool in plain words, AI builds it, and the teacher checks every fact against real sources before it reaches a class.

Try the three demos below.

Demo 1

The sliding map

Draft, pending consultation

Slide or press Play to watch the sea rise around Sahul: Australia, New Guinea and Tasmania joined as one land.

Land at that timeCoast todayPlaces

Drawn from today’s sea-floor depths. It shows the idea, not the exact coast at each time. Tap the map to enlarge it.

National Museum of Australia ↗Quaternary Science Reviews, 2018 ↗Sea-floor data (NOAA) ↗

How students use it

  • Students slide the map and describe what changes, and when.
  • They stop at one point in time and ask what the rising sea meant for the people living on that land.
  • They compare what the map shows with what it can’t show.

The drag-to-order timeline

Drag the cards into order, earliest at the top. Or use the ▲ ▼ buttons. Then check.

    Nature, 2017 ↗NMA: Lake Mungo ↗NMA: Tasmania ↗UNESCO: Budj Bim ↗NMA: First Fleet ↗

    How students use it

    • Students order the events from what they have read, before they check.
    • They look at the dates and the gaps between them, and say what a short list of events leaves out.
    • They explain one link between two cards: what came first, and what followed.

    Demo 3

    Read, Ask, Decide

    How long have people lived in Australia, and how do we know?

    1. Read: what is agreed

    Draft, pending consultation
    • All the sources here say First Nations people have lived in Australia for tens of thousands of years.
    • Scientists do not agree on exactly when people first arrived. One team says about 65,000 years ago. Another team says about 50,000 to 55,000 years ago.

    2. Ask: three witnesses, three questions

    1. 1. What did they say they saw?
    2. 2. Could they really have seen it?
    3. 3. Why might they say it?

    The pictures are silhouettes or drawings. No face has been invented. Tap a picture to make it bigger.

    The Budj Bim World Heritage listing

    UNESCO World Heritage Committee · 2019

    “For the Gunditjmara, deep time refers to the idea that they have always been there. From an archaeological perspective, deep time refers to a period of at least 32,000 years that Aboriginal people have lived in the Budj Bim Cultural Landscape.”

    In plain words: the Gunditjmara say they have always been there. Archaeologists say people have lived there for at least 32,000 years.

    3. Decide

    Pick the finding closest to yours.

    Your answers stay in this browser, on this device only. Nothing is sent anywhere.

    How students use it

    • Students read what is agreed, then question each witness with the same three questions.
    • They judge how far each account can be trusted, and why it might have been written.
    • They choose a finding and give a reason. “We can’t know” is always an option, so students practise saying when the records leave too much out.

    Safeguards

    • Real, cited sources only. No invented quotes or faces.
    • First Nations content is marked “Draft, pending consultation”.
    • No student data is collected. Answers stay in the student’s own browser.
